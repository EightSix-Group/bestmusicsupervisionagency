When to commission music instead of licensing a song

Commissioned music is written for your brand and your edit. The rights are agreed up front, and nobody else has the track.

Why commission

  • Music written for your brand can come with broad rights that you agree up front, and it does not depend on a song that may turn out to be impossible to clear. It takes time, so brief it before the edit locks, not after.

What to write into the brief

  • Media, territories and term, the number of cutdowns and formats (six-second bumpers, vertical social, retail screens, radio), the picture or the script, and the tone. A buyout covers only the media, territories and term written into it; after the term, the spot has to come off air or be renewed.

Rights and registration

  • Agree who owns the composition and the recording, which media and territories are covered, for how long, and whether the music is registered with a collecting society. For example, the Westwing sound logo by EightSix is GEMA-registered, with worldwide rights for all media and unlimited duration.

Alternatives to the original recording

  • A newly recorded cover avoids licensing the original master recording, as Sony Music Germany and Wise Music Creative both point out. You still need the publisher’s consent for the advertising use and any changes, plus the rights in the new recording and in the musicians’ performances, usually secured in the production contract.
  • A sound-alike is risky: a track that copies a melody can be an adaptation that needs the author’s consent. Commissioned music, songs by emerging artists and production libraries are the other routes. Check that a library licence covers advertising, your media and your territory.

Three questions for a custom music brief

Put these questions to every agency on your shortlist. This is how EightSix answers them.

Who owns and registers the music, and for how long can we use it?

  • The rights are agreed up front. The Westwing sound logo, for example, is GEMA-registered with worldwide rights for all media and unlimited duration.

Can one team compose, produce and clear the music?

  • Yes. For the Siemens iQ700 campaign EightSix delivered composition, music production, sound design and licensing in one project.

Do you work with artists or with libraries?

  • For bespoke music, EightSix works directly with artists and composers, and ArtistMatch™ finds new artists that fit the brand’s mDNA™ profile and budget. For ready-to-use tracks, Brand Studio adds a licensed catalogue.

When is custom music better than licensing a song?

When the song you want cannot be cleared, when the budget does not reach it, or when you want rights you can set up front and a track that no one else uses.

Who owns commissioned music?

That depends on the contract. Agree who owns the composition and the recording, which media and territories are covered, for how long, and whether the music is registered with a collecting society.

How long does it take to commission music for an ad?

It depends on the project. Brief the music before the edit locks. For comparison, GEMA says clearing rights for an audiovisual production can take several weeks or months.
