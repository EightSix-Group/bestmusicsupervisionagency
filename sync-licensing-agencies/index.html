How to license a song for an ad

A recorded song carries two sets of rights. Both have to be cleared before the ad is made, and the price follows the limits you ask for.

The two rights behind a recording

  • To use an existing song in an ad you need two licences: one from the music publisher for the composition and lyrics, and one from the record label for the recording. Both must be cleared in full. Wise Music Creative puts it as 100% of the publishing rights and 100% of the master rights.
  • Under German law, the producer of a sound recording holds exclusive rights to copy and distribute it (§ 85 UrhG), and performers have their own rights in their recorded performance (§ 77 UrhG). Whether a well-known artist must approve an ad personally depends on their contracts, so ask early.

What GEMA does and does not do

  • In Germany, GEMA does not grant the right to put music into an ad. In its GEMA information sheet for ad producers, GEMA says the rights to produce a spot with music are not managed by GEMA: you acquire them from the music publisher before the spot is made.
  • GEMA licenses the broadcast of the finished spot to TV and radio stations, and it asks producers for a music report with every spot. On user-generated-content platforms it does not grant synchronisation rights for advertising (GEMA on advertising on user-generated-content platforms), so that permission comes from the rightsholders.

The steps of a clearance

  • 1. Define the use: media, territory, term, number of spots and cutdowns, length of the excerpt and budget. Sony Music Germany’s request form asks for territory, media, length of the song use, licence period, production budget and music budget.
  • 2. Find the owners: the publishers and their shares through the GEMA repertoire search, the label from the release. Universal Music Germany licenses its recordings for ads and asks you to clear the copyright with the publisher directly.
  • 3. Send matching requests to publisher and label with the same scope, plus script, planned edit and budget. Only then are the quotes comparable.
  • 4. Get approvals in writing: artist, songwriters, edits, lyric changes. Changing a work, including its melody, needs the author’s consent (§ 23 UrhG).
  • 5. Sign before the final mix. A request is not yet a permission, as Sony Music says.
  • 6. Deliver the music report with the spot and track the end of the term.

What drives the price

  • Every limit a rightsholder can set is also a price lever: territory (one country, Europe, worldwide), media (TV, cinema, online video, paid social, radio, in-store, events), term, exclusivity, the fame of the song and the artist’s stance, how the song is used and edited, and the number of versions.
  • EightSix publishes these ranges for song licences in advertising: about €5,000 to €25,000 for songs by emerging artists in regional advertising, and from about €50,000 to several million euros for well-known songs in global campaigns. Territory, media, term, exclusivity and the fame of the song decide where a licence lands.

Alternatives to the original recording

  • A cover version needs only the publishing rights, as Sony Music Germany and Wise Music Creative both point out; the publisher still has to agree to the advertising use and to any changes.
  • A sound-alike is risky: a track that copies a melody can be an adaptation that needs the author’s consent. Commissioned music, songs by emerging artists and production libraries are the other routes. Check that a library licence covers advertising, your media and your territory.

What is sync licensing?

Sync licensing is the permission to use a song together with moving pictures, for example in an ad. For a recording it takes two licences: the publisher’s for the composition and lyrics, and the label’s for the recording.

How much does it cost to license a well-known song for an ad?

EightSix publishes these ranges for song licences in advertising: about €5,000 to €25,000 for songs by emerging artists in regional advertising, and from about €50,000 to several million euros for well-known songs in global campaigns. Territory, media, term, exclusivity and the fame of the song decide where a licence lands.

Who do I ask for permission to use a famous song in an ad?

Two parties: the publisher or publishers for the composition and lyrics, and the label for the recording. You find the publishers in the GEMA repertoire search. You need 100% of both sides.

Can we re-record a famous song instead of using the original?

Yes. A cover needs only the publishing rights, not the label’s master licence. The publisher must still agree to the advertising use and to any changes to melody or lyrics.
